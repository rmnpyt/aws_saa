## High Availability vs. Disaster Recovery

**High Availability (HA)**: The system remains operational during failures within the primary region by eliminating single points of failure.

**Disaster Recovery (DR)**: The ability to recover from a catastrophic event (entire region down, data corruption) with an acceptable level of data loss and downtime.

Both are separate concerns with different solutions, costs, and architectures.

## Two Critical Metrics

### RTO (Recovery Time Objective)
**How long can the business tolerate being down?**

- RTO of 1 hour: Acceptable to be offline for 1 hour during a disaster
- RTO of 15 minutes: Must be back online within 15 minutes
- RTO of 0: No downtime tolerance — requires active-active multi-region

Lower RTO = more infrastructure cost.

### RPO (Recovery Point Objective)
**How much data can the business afford to lose?**

- RPO of 24 hours: Acceptable to lose up to 24 hours of data
- RPO of 1 hour: Must replicate data at least every 60 minutes
- RPO of 0: Zero data loss tolerance — requires synchronous replication

Lower RPO = more replication overhead.

<KeyConcept title="RTO vs. RPO">
RTO is about **time** (how long can you be down). RPO is about **data** (how much data can you lose). A backup every 24 hours means RPO = 24 hours. Failover in 10 minutes means RTO = 10 minutes.
</KeyConcept>

## DR Strategies (Ordered by Cost and Recovery Speed)

### 1. Backup and Restore (cheapest, slowest)
- Back up data to S3 or cross-region
- No infrastructure running in the DR region
- To recover: provision all infrastructure + restore backups

**RTO**: Hours | **RPO**: Hours | **Cost**: Very low

**AWS tools**: S3 Cross-Region Replication, AWS Backup, AMI snapshots

### 2. Pilot Light (cold standby)
- Core infrastructure (database replication, core services) is always running in DR region
- Application servers are stopped or only run minimal instances
- To recover: launch application servers and scale up

**RTO**: 10s of minutes | **RPO**: Minutes | **Cost**: Low

**Key**: Database is always being replicated, so data is fresh.

### 3. Warm Standby
- Fully functional smaller-scale copy is always running in DR region
- Can handle limited traffic at reduced scale
- To recover: scale up the DR environment to full capacity

**RTO**: Minutes | **RPO**: Seconds to minutes | **Cost**: Medium

### 4. Multi-Site Active-Active (most expensive, fastest)
- Full capacity running in both (or more) regions simultaneously
- Traffic distributed across regions (Route 53 latency routing)
- A region failure just removes that region from traffic routing — no recovery needed

**RTO**: Near zero | **RPO**: Near zero | **Cost**: High (2x infrastructure)

## Multi-AZ vs. Multi-Region

| | Multi-AZ | Multi-Region |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope** | Protects against AZ failure | Protects against regional disaster |
| **Latency** | ~1 ms between AZs | ~10-100 ms between regions |
| **Data sync** | Synchronous (RDS Multi-AZ, EFS) | Often asynchronous |
| **Failover** | Automatic (RDS, ALB, EFS) | Usually requires manual trigger or Route 53 health checks |
| **Cost** | ~2x single-AZ | ~2x single-region + data transfer |
| **Primary use** | Standard HA (99.99% SLA) | Disaster recovery |

## AWS Availability and SLAs

- Individual AZs are designed for 99.99% availability (4 nines = ~53 min downtime/year)
- Multi-AZ deployments can achieve 99.99%+ at the application level
- Multi-region active-active can approach 99.999% (5 nines = ~5 min downtime/year)

## Resilience Best Practices

1. **Eliminate single points of failure**: Deploy across multiple AZs minimum
2. **Automate failover**: Use ALB health checks, RDS Multi-AZ auto-failover, Auto Scaling
3. **Decouple components**: SQS, SNS between services so one failure doesn't cascade
4. **Stateless compute**: Store session state in ElastiCache/DynamoDB so any instance can serve any request
5. **Data replication**: Synchronous for HA (Multi-AZ), asynchronous for DR (cross-region)
6. **Test your DR plan**: Run GameDays and chaos engineering exercises (AWS Fault Injection Simulator)

<ExamTip>
When an exam question mentions "an entire region fails" — think **Multi-Region DR** strategy. When it says "an AZ fails" — think **Multi-AZ** with automatic failover (ALB + Auto Scaling + RDS Multi-AZ).
</ExamTip>

## Summary

- **RTO** = max acceptable downtime. **RPO** = max acceptable data loss.
- **Backup/Restore**: Cheapest, slowest (hours RTO). Store backups cross-region.
- **Pilot Light**: Core infrastructure always running, app servers off. Minutes RTO.
- **Warm Standby**: Reduced capacity always running. Minutes RTO, seconds RPO.
- **Multi-Site Active-Active**: Full capacity both regions, near-zero RTO/RPO. Most expensive.
- Multi-AZ protects against AZ failures (automatic). Multi-Region protects against disasters.
